# Engagement on other people's posts

Replies are how a small or reset account meets people. On X, replies are
discovered in the conversation under the original post, and the code filters
out-of-network replies from For You (see [evidence](evidence.md) §3). So
replies earn profile visits, mutual follows and relationships. Those then
feed the ranking of the user's own posts (reply weight 5.0, mutual boost 15.0).

## The reply list

A private X List the user builds by hand. About 30 accounts:

| Tier | Share | Who | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bigger accounts | ~30% | Roughly 5x to 20x the user's size, same audience | Visibility under their posts |
| Peers | ~50% | Similar size, similar audience | They reply back; mutual follows form |
| Smaller, rising | ~20% | Thoughtful smaller accounts | Goodwill, early relationships |

The 5x to 20x band and the tier idea are operator advice (Reachmore,
hanamizuki x-growth "Dream 100"). UNVERIFIED as a rule; it's a sensible start.
Skip accounts that are mostly politics or ragebait.

## When

Early. Replies near the top of a fresh post get read. Operators say the first
15 to 60 minutes (UNVERIFIED). Some big accounts limit early replies; Bier
said in May 2026 he allows only in-network replies for the first 20 minutes
on his posts to block bots.

## Viral moments

Operator observation (Oct 2026, UNVERIFIED): a post from a bigger account in
the user's niche that is climbing fast (far above that account's usual
numbers within hours) is a short window for visibility.

- Only from accounts on the user's list or ones the user names; same lane.
- Early (first hour or so): a stand-alone reply that adds a step, an
  example or a sharper line.
- Late (many hours in, hundreds of replies): a reply is likely buried.
  A quote post with a real addition from the user's own experience can
  work better. Route quote copy through `x-post`.
- Skip if the topic is political, sensitive, or ragebait, however big it is.
- Viral replies sit after the user's own posts and regular reply list in
  priority, unless the user's context says otherwise.

## How many

Quality over volume. Bier (Jan 2026) called "reply hundreds of times a day"
a myth and said low-effort posts spend reach. Starting target: 5 to 15 real
replies a day. Some older skill packs say 20 to 50; that conflicts with X's
own guidance.

## Time ratio

Rebuilding an account: spend most X time on replies early. Operator rule of
thumb: about 70% replies, 30% posting under 3k followers, moving toward
50/50 later (hanamizuki x-growth). UNVERIFIED. Adjust from the weekly review.

## Good reply moves

A good reply stands alone: someone who reads only the reply gets something
and wants to click the name.

1. **Add the missing step.** "The part people skip here is what happens after the form fills."
2. **A real, specific example** (only if true). Use `{{EXAMPLE}}`.
3. **A respectful counterpoint.** "I'd push back a little. For a solo operator, a second tool usually adds a second place to forget."
4. **A sharper version** of their point in one line.
5. **A genuine question** that moves the thread forward. Not "thoughts?"

Rules: 1 to 3 sentences. No "Great post," "This," "100%," emoji-only, or "gm."
No pitching, links, or "DM me" under other people's posts. No dunking:
"ratioing" someone invites mutes and blocks, which carry heavy negative
weights. Disagree with ideas, never people.

## Bigger accounts and mutuals

- Reply for weeks before any ask. Quote their best ideas with a real addition and credit them.
- Never tag big accounts for attention or ask for a repost.
- Follow back peers who engage for real in the same lane; mutuals get the reply boost.
- Don't follow-for-follow; an unrelated follow graph muddies who sees the user's posts.
- Weekly: note three people who replied more than once and reply to their posts that week.
- A relationship can lead to an "earned" channel (podcast, collab) in Ryan's
  terms. The user decides; agents only draft.

## Output format

```
Original post: <one-line summary>
1. "<reply>" (move: add step | example | counterpoint | sharper | question)
   Why: <one line>
2. ...
Recommended: #n
Needs from the user: <any fact to confirm, or none>
```
