# Long post, thread, or Article

| Format | Use when | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Long post** (subscribers, up to 25,000 chars) | One story or breakdown, 6 to 15 lines | Hook line, then short paragraphs; the hook must work before "Show more" |
| **Thread** | Steps that each stand alone and could each get replies | Post 1 works alone; number the steps; last post closes the loop without a bait ask |
| **X Article** (Premium since Jan 7, 2026) | A deep piece tied to a story chapter or a long video; once a week or less | Clear-promise title, scannable sections, embedded posts or images where they help, cover image with one idea |

Threads vs long posts: X doesn't publish a comparison. INTERPRETATION: the
author-diversity decay and "each post uses some reach" both lean toward one
strong item over many weak ones. A long post keeps all engagement in one place.

Articles: Bier wrote in March 2026 that X "decided to reward Articles instead
of single posts" (context: a creator incentive). Treat that as a signal X
wants Articles, not a ranking rule. Ryan's "Distribution 101" is itself an
Article and names Articles as the X-native long format.

Titles: big promise, clear outcome, no confusion. Covers: their only job is
to get the click; less is more.

## Article packaging (plan it before writing)

Operator advice (Chan, Oct 26, 2026; UNVERIFIED): readers who click an
Article are self-selected, so the click decides the reach. Plan the
packaging first. If it can't be made clickable, pick another idea or format.

| Part | Job | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Banner** | Sell the outcome visually, or show the real work | A real photo of the user, their workspace or the actual thing built. One idea, readable on a phone. No generic AI art |
| **Title** | Sell the outcome the reader wants | Plain words, a specific outcome, no promise the user can't show |
| **Intro** | Answer the reader's biggest objection ("I'm not technical," "I don't have time") | First 2 to 4 lines; earns the scroll |

The three parts never repeat each other. Each does one job.

## Article types

- **Process Article:** exactly how the user does the work, step by step, so
  readers experience the expertise before they ever talk to the user. Best
  fit for accounts that aren't widely known yet.
- **Case study:** a real client or project from A to B. Needs a cleared
  `{{RESULT}}` and consent (see privacy).
- **Framework or SOP:** an internal checklist made public.
- **Expanded winner:** a post that outperformed, given the full depth.

## Ending

End with one CTA to a real `{{CTA}}`: another Article, a lead magnet, a
newsletter or a booking link, as the user's context allows. Readers who
finish are warm, so a direct next step fits better here than in a short
post. No engagement instructions.

## Distribution after publishing

Plan 2 to 3 quote posts of the user's own Article for the following weeks.
Each one uses a new angle: a different outcome, a different objection, a new
example, or a note for new followers. Each must stand alone and add
something; never repost the same text. The user posts them; route the copy
through `x-post`. Track each quote's clicks and profile visits separately.

## Links

Follow the shared link guidance and the user's link placement setting. Links
inside an Article body are part of the Article.
